Phone-first personal expense tracker for one user, in Danish kroner. The problem it solves is capture, not analysis: the design constraint that outranks everything is pocket to saved expense in under five seconds and no more than three taps, because previous budgets died of logging friction. Roughly 70% of the value is fast logging; the other 30% is a forward view of the month — scheduled bills, income dates, and how much is actually left on the 20th.

Stack: Expo SDK 57 / React Native / TypeScript with expo-router, local SQLite via raw SQL behind a Store interface, money as integer øre, fully offline. A Next.js PC web app plus sync API and Postgres are planned but not started.

## Notes

### Capture speed is the product `#9` 📌  [capture, product]
The one design constraint that outranks everything: pocket to saved expense in under five seconds and no more than three taps. Any feature that slows the capture path gets moved off it.

Roughly 70% of the value is fast logging, 30% is the forward-looking month view. Spreadsheets and receipt-hoarding failed because logging was tedious and the results were out of sight, so the habit died within a week or two. app/add.tsx and lib/amountInput.ts are the product — treat regressions there as critical.

### Rejected features — do not re-propose `#10` 📌  [product]
Voice entry: explicitly and permanently declined, including Assistant-triggered variants. Do not reintroduce it in any form.

Time-of-day category suggestions: the user's schedule is irregular enough (dinner at 07:00 is normal) that the guess would be wrong more often than right, and a wrong default is worse than no default.

Open-banking bank feed: checked and unavailable for this bank and country. Do not design features that assume an automatic transaction feed.

Business profile: dropped entirely. Business books live in Dinero and Skat; a convenient second ledger here would risk becoming a habit that bypasses the legal system of record. Dropping it also removed a mandatory column from every table and a control from every screen, so capture got faster.

Also out of scope: multi-user or household mode, investments and net worth, multi-currency beyond DKK.

### The widget is the unvalidated bet the stack rests on `#11` 📌  [android, architecture, widget]
The stack moved from Next.js to Expo for exactly one reason: a PWA cannot deliver an Android home screen widget — widgets are native RemoteViews, and an installed PWA gets a launcher icon and shortcuts, nothing more.

That this works from an Expo dev build has not been proven on this machine or this device, and the relevant libraries move fast enough that documentation may be stale. Nothing substantial should be built on top of it until the spike passes. Flutter is the fallback: more mature home_widget story, at the cost of learning Dart.

### Categories archive rather than delete, and are optional when logging `#17`  [product]
Removing a category sets archivedAt: it disappears from the numpad while every past entry keeps pointing at it, and it can be restored. Deleting would silently rewrite past months — categorised spending would become uncategorised and last month's totals would change retroactively. Historical numbers that move are worse than a slightly longer settings list.

An entry can also be saved with an amount and nothing else. Two taps beats three, and a logged expense with no category is far more valuable than an expense never logged because the right category was not obvious at the till. Uncategorised spending gets its own row in the breakdown rather than being hidden. The planned batch tidy-up screen is what cleans this up later.

Related invariant for when transfers arrive: transfers between own accounts are a distinct entry type and must never count as spending.

### Notification capture is additive, never the only path `#18`  [android, capture]
Google Wallet covers most in-person payments and MobilePay may or may not emit a usable notification. Online card payments, bills and account transfers produce nothing at all.

Captured notifications land in a pending inbox for one-tap confirmation; manual entry stays the primary, always-available path. Designing the app around notification capture would silently lose whole classes of spending — exactly the invisible spending the app exists to make visible.

### Open question — which screen the app opens to `#19`  [product, ui]
Opening straight to the numpad is fastest to log. Opening to the overview puts the budget in front of the user on every launch, which is the stated reason previous budgets failed — they were out of sight.

Current proposal: the app icon opens the overview, the widget opens the numpad directly, and a setting overrides the default. Needs real use to settle; revisit after two weeks of daily use.

### Open question — how often to nudge a balance re-anchor `#20`  [product]
'How much will I have on the 20th?' requires knowing the balance now, and with no bank feed available the user has to type it in. One balance figure, updated whenever convenient, with everything projecting from it; re-anchoring corrects any accumulated drift, so the cost of it being stale is bounded.

Open: how often to nudge for a re-anchor without it becoming another chore. Weekly feels right, but it is untested.

### Browser preview persists to localStorage, not SQLite `#21`  [tech-debt]
expo-sqlite on web needs a WASM build and COOP/COEP headers on the dev server — a fragile dependency for what is currently the only working iteration loop — so the web build uses a localStorage driver behind the same Store interface (db/webStore.ts).

Only persistence differs; all aggregation lives in lib/ and operates on plain objects, so both drivers share identical logic. The consequence: the browser cannot validate anything database-specific. Test schema and migration behaviour on device.

### Build environment — no local Android toolchain `#22`  [android, tooling]
This machine has no JDK and no Android SDK (java not found, ANDROID_HOME unset), so `npx expo run:android` is not an option without installing Android Studio and a JDK first. Device builds go through EAS in the cloud instead; eas.json is already configured with a development profile.

Anything with native code — the widget, the notification listener — needs a dev build, not Expo Go. The APK only needs rebuilding when native code changes; after that the device loop is `npx expo start --dev-client`.

The day-to-day loop today is the browser: `npx expo start --web` on localhost:8081, wired up in .claude/launch.json.

### Money is integer øre everywhere `#12`  [architecture]
Amounts are stored, passed and computed as an integer number of øre. Floats appear nowhere, at any layer — a finance app that is occasionally off by a øre is worse than useless.

Formatting is hand-rolled in lib/money.ts rather than using Intl, so output is identical on web, on device and in tests, with no dependency on the JS engine's locale data (Hermes and browsers differ). Danish output: 1.234,50 kr., Monday-first weeks, Europe/Copenhagen dates.

### Four layers, and code does not skip one `#13`  [architecture]
db/ is the only place that speaks SQL, exposing a Store interface that returns plain objects. lib/ is pure functions — money formatting, date maths, the numpad state machine, month aggregation — with no I/O and no React; this is what the tests cover. state/ binds the store to React and holds the ledger in memory. app/ is expo-router screens: presentation only, no SQL and no money arithmetic.

The whole ledger loads into memory deliberately. A few thousand rows over several years is nothing, and it means every total is a pure function in lib/ rather than a SQL query — far easier to test and identical on every platform. Screens must not hard-code hex values; colours and spacing come from theme/tokens.ts. Schema changes ship as a new entry in db/migrations.ts — never edit an existing one.

### Sync model — append-only ledger, last-write-wins `#14`  [architecture, sync]
Every row carries a client-generated UUID, an updated_at timestamp and a deleted_at soft delete. Sync pushes rows newer than the last watermark and pulls the same in reverse; conflicts resolve by newest updated_at and are written to a visible conflict log.

There is exactly one user with at most two devices, so the hard part of sync — concurrent edits by different people — does not exist, and CRDTs would be complexity bought for a problem this project does not have. Soft deletes matter because a hard-deleted row cannot be distinguished from a row the other device has not seen yet.

The phone's SQLite database is the source of truth for the phone; the server is a sync peer, not an authority. The phone works fully offline, always — the network is never on the critical path for logging an expense.

### Encryption deferred but designed for `#15`  [architecture, security]
End-to-end encryption is wanted but adds real complexity, especially for a browser client that needs the key in memory. Decision for v1: TLS in transit, encryption at rest on the server, strong auth — and the sensitive fields (amount, note, merchant) stored in a single payload column rather than as separate typed columns.

Swapping that column from plaintext JSON to a ciphertext blob then needs no schema migration, because every query that filters or aggregates on those fields runs locally on the phone anyway. Shipping the habit-forming part matters more than perfect crypto on day one, but a data model that forecloses encryption would be an expensive mistake later.

Hard rule regardless: never store card numbers, bank credentials or CPR numbers. Not now, not later.

### Budget covers variable spending only `#16`  [product]
Rent, insurance and subscriptions are not part of the monthly budget figure — they are scheduled items handled by the calendar and the forward projection. The budget applies only to discretionary and variable spending.

Mixing them makes the number meaningless: a 10.000 kr. budget that is 75% consumed by rent on the first of the month tells the user nothing actionable. Separated, 'left to spend' is a number that can actually change behaviour today.
